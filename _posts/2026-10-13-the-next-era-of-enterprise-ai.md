# The Next Enterprise AI Revolution: From Buying Software to Building Intelligence

**For decades, enterprises have bought software to run their businesses. AI is creating an opportunity to build intelligence around how their businesses actually work.**

I believe this is one of the most significant shifts in enterprise technology strategy in decades—particularly for companies outside the technology sector.

### The old paradigm: Powerful software, fragmented intelligence

For years, the dominant enterprise technology strategy has been *buy before build*.

Companies invested in sophisticated applications for HR, R&D, supply chain, manufacturing, finance, and commercial operations. These systems brought tremendous value, but often reinforced organizational silos.

Each application optimized part of the business. Yet knowledge, decisions, and workflows remained fragmented across organizational boundaries.

Enterprises became increasingly digitized, but not necessarily more interconnected or intelligent.

### What AI changes: The ability to build

AI is fundamentally changing the economics and accessibility of software development.

AI-assisted development enables smaller teams to build applications, integrate tools, and automate workflows at a speed and scale that previously required much greater engineering resources.

This creates a new opportunity for non-technology enterprises.

For the first time, building interconnected, company-specific intelligent systems is becoming realistic for a much broader range of organizations—not just technology companies with large software engineering teams.

And the biggest opportunity is not replacing individual SaaS applications with cheaper alternatives.

**It is building an intelligence layer that connects the applications, proprietary knowledge, business processes, and decisions across the enterprise.**

### From isolated applications to interconnected enterprise intelligence

Imagine an enterprise where R&D, supply chain, manufacturing, and commercial teams no longer depend primarily on employees manually transferring information and context between disconnected systems.

Instead, an interconnected intelligence layer can:

- Connect proprietary knowledge and data across functions.
- Coordinate AI agents, existing applications, and business workflows.
- Support decisions using shared business context.
- Execute approved actions across organizational boundaries.

The opportunity is not simply to automate existing tasks. It is to transform how the organization operates as a connected system.

Through my own experience building enterprise AI platforms and agentic systems, I have increasingly come to believe that this is where some of the greatest long-term value of enterprise AI will emerge.

The technology is becoming more accessible. But understanding the company's proprietary knowledge, workflows, organizational dependencies, and decision-making remains difficult for external vendors to replicate.

**That organizational context is becoming a strategic asset—and AI offers a new way to turn it into operational intelligence.**

### A new strategic question for enterprise leaders

This does not mean SaaS is disappearing, or that enterprises should build everything themselves.

Established software platforms will remain essential for standardized capabilities, infrastructure, and systems of record.

Building interconnected intelligence also requires deliberate investment in architecture, security, data access, governance, and internal engineering capabilities.

But the strategic question is changing.

For decades, enterprise leaders have asked: *Should we build or buy this software?*

Going forward, a more consequential question may be:

**Which parts of our enterprise intelligence should we own—and how do we connect them to create a lasting competitive advantage?**

I believe the enterprises that answer this question well will move beyond deploying AI tools toward building something far more valuable: **their own interconnected, intelligent enterprise.**

What do you think? Will AI fundamentally change what non-technology enterprises should build and own?
